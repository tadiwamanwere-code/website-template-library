Chapter 01

Arrival

The Twin Otter banks once over the channel so that you see the whole island at the same moment — 148 hectares, one jetty, no roads. Thirty-five minutes earlier you were still on the mainland, holding a boarding pass.

35 min · de Havilland Twin Otter · 09:40 & 14:20 daily
Chapter 02

The Villas

Twenty-two roofs along 4.1 kilometres of shoreline. Coconut timber, coral-lime plaster, ceiling fans where other resorts put walls. Every villa faces either west or east — you choose which end of the day you would rather own.

22 villas · 190–1,120 sqm · every one with a pool
Chapter 03

The Reef

The house reef begins forty metres off Loka Beach and falls away to twenty-eight at the wall. Between June and September the mantas queue at Vaka Cleaning Station — eleven of them one morning last August, counted from the surface.

18 dive sites · 28.4°C water · 30 m visibility
Chapter 04

The Table

Tāla cooks over coral-wood fire from four in the afternoon, and by seven the whole terrace smells of it. Or take the Lantern Table out to the sandbank at low tide — two hours, one candle each, and the boat comes back for you.

4 venues · 4,200-bottle cellar · reef-to-table

The Island

One and a half square kilometres of deliberate inconvenience

Alaló was a copra plantation until 1974 and a bird sanctuary after that. We took the lease in 2016 and opened, slowly, three years later.

“The island only makes sense if it is difficult to reach. Every other decision followed from that one.”

Iva Mesari — founder, on the 2016 lease

There are no clocks in the villas and no televisions in any of them. The generator field is solar with a 2.4 MWh battery, and the desalination plant makes 90,000 litres a day — enough that nothing arrives in plastic. The last boat of supplies comes in on Tuesdays.

Operations note · updated March 2026

Ma'ari Spa

Four treatment pavilions on stilts over the north lagoon, a hydrothermal circuit cut into the coral-stone ridge, and a 24-metre seawater vitality pool. Oils are tamanu and monoi, pressed on the island.

The Kaimana Reef Project

Two resident marine biologists keep 6,400 coral fragments across 18 nursery frames off Point Ualu. Guests can plant a frame; survivorship figures are published every March, whatever they say.

The Salt Room

A cellar cut nine metres into the ridge — 4,200 bottles held at a steady 13°C without a compressor, because the rock does it for us. Tastings at 18:00, Tuesdays and Fridays.
