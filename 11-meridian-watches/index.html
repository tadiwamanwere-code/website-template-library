Chapter 03

The second, held still

Every conventional wristwatch smears the second across six balance beats. The MC-7 stores those beats and releases them whole.

Fig. 02 — Flirt & star, section
Fig. 02 — Dead-beat module Scale 6 : 1
The star turns with the fourth wheel and holds the flirt under tension. Once per revolution of the star the flirt slips the arm, snaps 6° under its own spiral, and the seconds pinion advances one whole division. Cost to the going train: 2 h 10 min of reserve.

A sweeping hand is an approximation. Ours refuses to round anything off.

§ 03.1 — Why it steps

The balance of the MC-7 oscillates at 3 Hz — six vibrations, six escapement releases, every second. In an ordinary watch each of those releases nudges the seconds hand forward by a sixth of a division, so what you actually read is a blur that only averages a second.

The seconde morte takes a second branch off the fourth wheel. A two-armed steel star, hardened to 58 HRC and mirror-polished on its flanks, winds a light spiral spring on the flirt through 180° of its travel. At the release point the arm falls away and the flirt discharges in under 12 ms, carrying the seconds pinion exactly one division and stopping dead against its banking pin.

You hear it before you see it: a dry, single report rather than the whisper of a free train.

§ 03.2 — What it costs

The module is parasitic. Winding a spring sixty times a minute takes torque that would otherwise be running the escapement, so we build the reserve back with two barrels coupled in series — a flatter torque curve across 72 hours rather than a taller one across 40.

Amplitude at full wind is 297° dial-up, and we accept nothing below 255° at the 60-hour mark. Each movement is regulated in six positions across 21 days on the bench in Le Sentier before it ever sees a case.

Élise Charrat, who cuts and finishes every flirt herself, will assemble roughly five movements a month. That is the ceiling, and it is the reason the run is capped at 180 pieces.

Fig. 03 — Bench 4, Le Sentier

Flirt blanks are cut from 0.42 mm blued spring steel, filed to profile against a brass template, then poised on a Bergeon truing calliper until the arm holds level at any angle. Six blanks are made for every one that is kept.

Côtes de Genève

Struck on the bridges with a boxwood peg under a rotating abrasive, 0.9 mm pitch, each stripe laid in one unbroken pass so it never doubles back on itself. The plate is re-frosted and re-struck if a single stripe wanders.

Anglage

Every bridge edge bevelled at 45° with a jacot file, then burnished with gentian wood until it throws a clean line of light. There are nine inward angles in this calibre — a rotating tool cannot cut an inward angle, which is exactly why we specify them.

Inward angles9

Perlage

Overlapping circular graining across the mainplate — 0.6 mm pearls at 60% overlap, laid on a strict rectilinear grid rather than following the plate outline. Roughly 1 940 impressions, none of them repeated.

Poli noir

The click, the flirt and the escape-wheel bridge are black-polished: rubbed on a tin plate with diamantine until the surface is flat to within a wavelength of light and reads as pure black from one angle, pure white from the next.

Azurage

The seconds register is snailed on a rose engine turning at 220 rpm — 84 concentric grooves between the centre and the minute track, cut in a single sitting because the tool cannot be reset mid-dial without leaving a witness line.

Bleuissage

Hands and screws are heat-blued over a bed of brass filings and drawn to 290 °C, then quenched at the instant the straw yellow turns. No chemical blue, no coating — the colour is an oxide 40 nm thick and it will never chip.
