002 — The Six Movements Autumn service · Nov 2026
Movement 01 / 06

先付

Hokkaidō scallop, brown-butter dashi

Raw, cut thick, warmed only by the broth poured over it at the counter. Finger lime for acid. Six weeks of kombu in that dashi.

Paired · Tedorigawa Yamahai Junmai, Ishikawa
Movement 02 / 06

御造り

Kinmedai aged eight days

Golden eye snapper, hung in the ageing cabinet behind you until the flesh turns translucent. Salt-cured plum, and nothing else on the plate.

Paired · Meursault Les Narvaux 2019, Boyer-Martenot
Movement 03 / 06

炭焼き

Nodoguro over binchōtan

Blackthroat seaperch, skin side down over white charcoal at 900°C for ninety seconds. Burnt scallion oil, brushed twice. You will hear it before you see it.

Paired · Hōraisen Wa Junmai Ginjō, Aichi
Movement 04 / 06

椀物

Clear broth, matsutake and hamo

The quiet one. Pine mushroom from Nagano, conger eel bone-cut three hundred times to the inch. Served in lacquer, lid on, so the steam arrives with it.

Paired · Hōjicha, first flush, brewed at 82°C
Movement 05 / 06

握り

Twelve pieces, cut to order

Akazu rice at body temperature, seasoned with three-year red vinegar. Chef Oyama forms each piece in front of you and expects you to eat it inside thirty seconds.

Paired · Kokuryū Kuzuryū Junmai, served warm at 45°C
Movement 06 / 06

水菓子

Kuromitsu, black sesame, toasted rice

Ice cream churned from the rice we toasted for the evening's tea. Okinawan black sugar syrup, poured at the table. Then the lights come up.

Paired · Kijōshu, ten years in cedar, 30ml
003 — The Counter

Rina Oyama

“I spent eleven years being told the fish was not ready. Now I am the one who says it.”

Oyama learned her knife work at Sushi Kaneda in Susukino, Sapporo — eleven winters, the last four running the second counter. She moved to London in 2017 for a six-month stage that she never went home from.

Kuroba opened in the spring of 2019 in a former silk-weaver's front room on Fournier Street: one slab of two-hundred-year-old hinoki, fourteen chairs, and a charcoal grill she had shipped from Wakayama in three pieces. There is no pass, no printed menu and no second sitting of the same food — the 18:00 and 20:45 services are cooked differently because the fish has changed by then.

She buys day-boat fish from Brixham and Peterhead on Monday and Thursday, and holds most of it. The ageing cabinet behind the counter is deliberately in view: guests are meant to watch their own dinner getting older.

The room

004 — Four things worth arriving early for
i1826

The hinoki slab

Four and a half metres of Japanese cypress, unfinished. It is sanded with water, never oiled, and it will be pale again by spring.

ii900°C

The binchōtan

White oak charcoal from Kishū, lit at 15:40 every service day. It takes ninety minutes to reach temperature and burns without smoke or flame.

iii2 — 21 days

The ageing cabinet

Glass-fronted, 1°C, 72% humidity. Turbot at four days, kinmedai at eight, and one obstinate amberjack that has been in there since the 14th.

iv61 labels

The cold room

Sixty-one sake, eleven of them koshu aged past a decade, and a short, stubborn list of Jura and Mosel that our sommelier Théo Marchand refuses to expand.

005 — Before you book

Tuesday to Saturday, two services: 18:00 and 20:45. Bookings open at 10:00 on the first Monday of each month for the month after next, and the November counter has historically gone in under nine minutes.

Tickets are prepaid in full. We hold four seats back from each Saturday release for the waiting list, which currently runs to 1,340 names.

Tell us at least 72 hours ahead and we will cook around shellfish, dairy and gluten without substituting the movement structure. We cannot cook a menu without fish — the entire kitchen is built around one grill and one ageing cabinet.

The counter is not suitable for guests under twelve.

Cancel more than seven days out and we refund in full. Inside seven days the seat is yours to transfer to anyone you like — email the name to the counter and we will change the setting.

If we cannot fill a same-day cancellation, the seat stays empty. We would rather cook for thirteen than rush someone into a chair.

Corkage is £45 a bottle, one bottle per pair, nothing we already list. Photographs are fine before the nigiri begins and not after — by then the rice is at temperature and thirty seconds matters more than the picture.

The room seats fourteen strangers side by side. Most nights they end up talking. That is not an accident.
