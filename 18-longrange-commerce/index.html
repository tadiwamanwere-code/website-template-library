The kiln at Vejle — 01 / 03

Clay, weighed
and rested

Every batch starts as 340kg of Jutland stoneware clay, wedged and then rested for eleven days before a single piece is thrown. Skipping that rest is why cheap stoneware crazes in its second year.

Batch
340 kg
Rest
11 days

Thrown, then
left alone

Four throwers, one wheel each, roughly ninety pieces a day between them. Nothing is slip-cast. The foot of every plate is trimmed by the same hand that threw it, which is why no two stacks sit perfectly flat.

Fired twice
at 1,280°C

A bisque firing, then glaze, then a second firing to vitrification. The cycle takes 38 hours and about one piece in fourteen comes out wrong. Those go to the seconds table rather than the skip.

Materials & care

Read this before
the dishwasher.

Vitrified at 1,280°C, so it takes the dishwasher, the oven to 220°C and the freezer without complaint. It will not take a cold plate under a hot grill — that is thermal shock and no glaze survives it. The bone glaze lightens slightly over the first year of use; that is the clay showing through, and it is not a fault.

Pans arrive pre-seasoned with linseed oil, three coats, baked on. Wash with hot water and a stiff brush, dry on the hob, wipe with a film of oil. Soap will not ruin the seasoning; leaving the pan wet overnight will. Rust is recoverable — steel wool, re-season, carry on.

Blown in soda-lime glass near Lyon, so each tumbler carries a faint seam and a slightly different weight. Dishwasher-safe on a normal cycle; avoid the intensive programme, which etches the surface over a few hundred washes. Bubbles in the wall are evidence of the mouth, not a defect.

Grown in Normandy, spun and woven in Guimarães, stonewashed twice so it arrives soft rather than starched. Wash at 40°C with like colours, tumble low, and do not iron unless you want the crease sharp. Flax improves for roughly forty washes and then holds steady for years.

Lids, handles, casserole knobs, apron straps and the boards' rubber feet are all sold on their own. If one piece of a set breaks, buy that piece — we hold spares for ten years after a range is discontinued. Email the workshop a photograph and we will tell you which part you need.
